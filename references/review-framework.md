# Review Framework

## Review Position

Use the employer-side HR compliance perspective. The goal is to help the employer decide whether the document is legally usable, operationally executable, cost-controlled, and not unnecessarily unfavorable to the employer.

## Inputs to Identify First

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Country / region | Determines mandatory labor contract rules and statutory entitlements |
| Employee type | Local hire, EOR employee, expatriate, contractor, intern, fixed-term employee, etc. |
| Employment model | Own entity, EOR, payroll provider, host entity, China entity assignment |
| Legal employer | Determines who signs, pays, withholds, terminates, and bears employer duties |
| Actual work location | May trigger mandatory local law, work authorization, tax, and social security |
| Governing law / jurisdiction | Must be checked against mandatory local employment law |
| Payroll location | Affects wage payment, withholding, currency, payslip, and social security handling |
| Visa / work authorization | Illegal work risk can override contract language |
| Document set | Contract, offer, annex, handbook, equity plan, commission plan, EOR terms |

## Clause Review Checklist

| Area | Employer-side review questions |
|---|---|
| Parties and employer identity | Is the legal employer correct? Is the entity licensed/registered where needed? Does an EOR role create ambiguity? |
| Position and work location | Is location flexible enough for business needs? Does remote or cross-border work create tax/visa risk? |
| Term and contract type | Is fixed-term or indefinite term valid locally? Are renewal and end-date rules clear? |
| Probation | Is probation allowed, within maximum duration, and linked to termination rights? |
| Compensation | Are salary, currency, frequency, bonus, commission, allowances, and deductions clear and locally lawful? |
| Working time and overtime | Are hours, rest days, overtime approval, and overtime pay compliant and cost-controlled? |
| Leave and benefits | Does the contract meet statutory leave and benefit requirements? Does it grant above-statutory rights? |
| Tax and social security | Is responsibility clear? Are withholding and employer contributions handled lawfully? |
| Visa / work authorization | Does employment depend on valid work authorization? Are employee obligations and termination triggers clear? |
| Policies and handbook | Are company policies incorporated without freezing future amendment rights? |
| Confidentiality and IP | Are confidentiality, data, inventions, and IP assignment enforceable locally? |
| Restrictive covenants | Are non-compete, non-solicit, compensation, duration, scope, and enforcement rules checked? |
| Termination | Are notice, cause, severance, garden leave, payment in lieu, and procedural requirements compliant? |
| Dispute resolution | Is forum/law enforceable? Does arbitration or court selection conflict with mandatory local labor rules? |
| Language and execution | Is controlling language clear? Are signatures, witnesses, wet ink/e-signature, and local filing needs handled? |

## Four Mandatory Judgments

For each material clause, answer all four:

1. **违法违规风险**: Is the clause illegal, invalid, missing, or inconsistent with mandatory local law?
2. **法定必备内容**: Does the contract include the mandatory contents required by the country/region?
3. **高于法定权益**: Does the contract grant employee rights, benefits, compensation, leave, notice, severance, or procedural protections above the statutory floor?
4. **雇主不利条款**: Does the clause increase cost, reduce flexibility, weaken evidence, create automatic entitlement, restrict termination, or bind future company policy changes?

## Recommended Review Sequence

1. Extract facts and quote short clause identifiers, not long contract passages.
2. Build or load the country legal baseline.
3. Compare contract clauses against statutory minimums and mandatory terms.
4. Identify above-statutory employee benefits and employer-unfavorable provisions.
5. Convert each issue into an HR action: revise, confirm, approve exception, accept risk, or escalate.
6. Separate confirmed findings from assumptions and items to be confirmed.
