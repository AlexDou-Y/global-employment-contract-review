# Political and Sovereignty Terminology Review

## Review Position

Review political, sovereignty, country/region, government, and legal-system terminology as a separate employer-side HR compliance and corporate governance issue. Do not treat it as a pure labor-law validity issue.

For China-headquartered or China-controlled employers, flag wording that may conflict with the company's country/region naming standards, One-China-related external communication requirements, government relations expectations, public filing standards, or internal approval rules.

## Mandatory Distinctions

When a clause contains sensitive political or sovereignty wording, separate these judgments:

1. **Local legal effectiveness**: whether the wording is needed or commonly used under the local legal system.
2. **Company political/compliance acceptability**: whether the wording is acceptable for a China-context employer, group template, internal approval, external filing, or cross-border document circulation.
3. **Operational alternative**: whether the same legal effect can be achieved with neutral country/region wording.
4. **Escalation need**: whether Legal, local counsel, government affairs, EOR/vendor, or the business owner must confirm before signing.

Do not state that a politically sensitive phrase is locally invalid unless there is a legal source supporting that conclusion. If the concern is company-side political/compliance positioning, say so explicitly.

## Mandatory Scan Areas

Scan at least these parts of the contract and annexes:

- parties, legal employer, affiliate definitions, address blocks, and company group descriptions;
- governing law, jurisdiction, dispute resolution, court/arbitration institution, and language clauses;
- work location, mobility, remote work, assignment, expatriate, and travel clauses;
- tax, social security, payroll, immigration, statutory benefit, and government filing clauses;
- country/region lists in confidentiality, data transfer, export control, sanctions, insurance, equity, and policy clauses;
- signature blocks, seals, certificates, exhibits, and employee-facing notices.

## Trigger Phrases and Patterns

Flag these patterns for review. The list is illustrative, not exhaustive.

| Pattern | Why to flag | Recommended handling |
|---|---|---|
| Taiwan-related clauses using `Republic of China`, `R.O.C.`, `中华民国`, or `中華民國` as a political/state reference | May be locally conventional in Taiwan legal documents, but can create China-context political wording and internal approval risk | Prefer neutral wording such as `台湾地区现行有效劳动及相关法律法规` where legally workable; confirm with Legal/local counsel if local enforceability requires the original wording |
| Treating Taiwan, Hong Kong, or Macau as a `country`, `state`, `nation`, or equivalent sovereign category | May conflict with China-context country/region naming standards | Change to `国家/地区`, `country/region`, `jurisdiction`, or a company-approved naming list |
| Listing territories or disputed areas as independent countries without company-approved naming standards | Creates external communication and governance risk | Use the company's approved country/region taxonomy; escalate if no standard exists |
| References to a government, court, regulator, statute, gazette, nationality, passport, or public authority that imply a sovereignty position | May be necessary locally but sensitive in cross-border templates | Preserve legal precision only where required; otherwise use neutral jurisdiction wording and confirm with Legal/local counsel |
| Inconsistent naming across Chinese, English, and local-language versions | Can create inconsistent legal and political interpretations | Align controlling-language terms and add a translation consistency note |

## Taiwan Governing-Law Example

If a Taiwan employment contract says `本协议受中华民国现行法律管辖` or `本協議受中華民國現行法律管轄`, do not ignore it.

Recommended issue judgment:

- The phrase may reflect local Taiwan legal drafting practice, so it should not automatically be described as invalid under Taiwan law.
- For a China-context employer, the expression `中华民国 / 中華民國 / Republic of China` should be flagged as political/sovereignty-sensitive wording.
- Recommend replacing it with a neutral formulation if local counsel confirms it preserves the intended governing-law effect, for example: `本协议受台湾地区现行有效劳动及相关法律法规管辖`.
- If the EOR, local counsel, court/arbitration practice, or filing authority requires the original term, list it as a signing-before-confirmation item and require Legal/local counsel risk acceptance.

## Risk Classification

Use these default classifications unless local facts justify a different level:

| Situation | Default level | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Wording conflicts with known company country/region naming standards or China-context external communication rules | 馃煚 | Confirm or adjust before signature |
| Wording may be locally necessary but politically sensitive for the employer | 馃煚 | Legal/local counsel confirmation and risk acceptance |
| Wording is inconsistent across contract language versions or annexes | 馃煛 | Align wording and controlling-language clause |
| Contract lacks a neutral country/region taxonomy where multiple jurisdictions are listed | 馃煝 | Add company-approved naming standard or drafting note |
| Wording creates clear regulatory, filing, sanctions, or public disclosure exposure supported by sources | 馃敶 | Must revise or escalate before signature |

## Marker

Use `⚑` as the report marker for political, sovereignty, and country/region terminology issues.

`⚑` is not a severity level. Pair it with the normal risk level when needed, for example:

- `🟠 ⚑ 政治/主权敏感表述：台湾地区适用法律表述`
- `🟡 ⚑ 国家/地区称谓不一致`
- `🔴 ⚑ 对外披露或监管文件存在明确敏感表述风险`

Do not place these findings under a heading that only describes employee benefits or above-statutory entitlements. If the report template has no standalone section, use a separate subsection titled `⚑ 政治/主权/地域称谓敏感表述专项提醒`, or broaden the `🟠` section heading to `重要雇主风险 / 签前确认事项`.

## Report Writing Rules

When reporting this issue:

- Put the finding under a standalone `⚑` section when the report includes one. Otherwise place it under the appropriate severity section and add the `⚑` marker to the finding title.
- Use a title such as `🟠 ⚑ 政治/主权敏感表述：台湾地区适用法律表述`.
- In `问题判断`, state both the local-law drafting context and the China-context employer risk.
- In `雇主影响`, explain approval, external communication, employee dispute, template reuse, and group governance impact.
- In `修改建议`, provide neutral wording and name the confirmer: Legal, local counsel, EOR/vendor, government affairs, or business owner.
- In `依据`, cite contract text as evidence. Add official or company-policy sources only when actually available. If no official/company source is available, mark as employer-side governance concern and move confirmation to `签署前待确认事项`.
